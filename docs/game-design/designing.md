# Designing the game

## Start from what already works

Hit Roblox games are mostly the same building blocks rearranged. Pick one to three proven reference games and break them down:

- **Fantasy:** what the player gets to be or do. It also decides the audience.
- **Unique mechanic:** the one thing that sets the game apart.
- **Core loop:** the action cycle the player repeats.
- **Tension:** where the "I barely made it" feeling comes from, if anywhere.
- **Progression:** what gets better in the short term and the long term.
- **Map:** its shape and how players move through it.

Then **change about 20%,** focused on the fantasy and the unique mechanic. Every change needs a reason.

- Copy how proven systems *work*, not how they *look*. Players already know them, so onboarding gets easier.
- Prefer a twist that fits the theme over the generic mechanic every game has.
- Avoid seasonal ideas and dead memes.

## What makes a game catch on

1. **A clickable fantasy:** something a kid would daydream about. Someone should want to click the moment they see it.
2. **A great first minute** (see `onboarding.md`).
3. **Better with friends.**
4. **A simple mechanic with depth:** easy to learn, and it plays differently every session.
5. **Clippable moments:** things people want to record and share.

Also:

- **Sunk cost:** give players something to invest in, so they come back.
- **A single mechanic isn't a game.** Wrap it in a fantasy and a progression.
- **Choose fantasy over realism** when realism gets in the way of fun.
- **Go big.** First builds are usually too easy and too sparse, more like a tech demo than a game.

## Every system must earn its place

- For every feature, ask: **why would a player want to do this?** If the payoff isn't visible, cut it.
- Don't add stock systems (rebirth, quests, daily counters) out of habit. Add them when the design needs them.
- Keep the hands-on interactions that make up the fantasy. Remove the busywork around them, and don't add steps to actions players repeat often.
- When skill is involved, skill changes the outcome.
- Make results obvious without domain knowledge.
- Never start an action the player didn't choose.
- Numbers on screen always match what the player sees.

## Social

- Design for friend groups, so no one is left out and everyone has their own agency.
- **Social flex:** progress others can see.
- Players want to progress on their own. Don't make them stop to watch someone else.

## The design doc: `GAME.md`

Before building, write a one-page `GAME.md` at the repo root:

- Title and fantasy
- Reference games, and what we take from each
- The twist
- The core loop, step by step
- The first minute
- How progression and money work
- What is out of scope

`GAME.md` is for you and later agents. The user doesn't read long documents, so don't send it. Send them a brief overview instead, a few short lines: the game in one sentence, the reference and the twist, and the core loop. Then wait for their OK before building. Keep `GAME.md` up to date as the game changes.
